## What to Do When Your AC Stops Working in Morris County

Start with the thermostat. Make sure it is set to cool, the temperature setting is lower than the room temperature, and the display is active. If the screen is blank, replace the batteries if your thermostat uses them, then check whether the HVAC breaker is on.

Next, check the breaker panel. A tripped AC breaker can happen during peak summer demand, especially when an older unit is working hard. Reset it once. If it trips again, stop there and call a technician, because repeated breaker trips can point to a failing motor, compressor, capacitor, or electrical issue.

Look at the air filter and outdoor unit. A clogged filter can restrict airflow until the indoor coil freezes. Outside, make sure the condenser is not covered by leaves, grass clippings, or patio furniture. Do not open electrical panels, force the fan blade, chip ice off the coil, or keep restarting a system that is making buzzing, grinding, or burning smells.

Call immediately if the AC is not cooling during extreme heat, someone in the home is elderly or medically vulnerable, the breaker keeps tripping, you see ice on the refrigerant line, or the outdoor unit is silent while the indoor blower runs. ## Most Common Reasons AC Stops Working in NJ Summer

Capacitor failure is one of the most common summer breakdowns. The capacitor helps start the compressor and fan motors. When it fails, you may hear humming, clicking, or a quick start-and-stop sound from the outdoor unit. This is often a same-day repair when the rest of the system tests properly.

Low refrigerant usually means there is a leak. Symptoms include warm air, ice on the lines, long run times, and poor humidity control. Adding refrigerant without diagnosing the leak is only a temporary move, so our technicians check pressures and system performance before recommending the right repair.

A tripped breaker can be a simple reset, but it can also be a warning. If the AC breaker trips again after one reset, there may be an electrical overload, short, weak capacitor, failing motor, or compressor problem. That is the point where a licensed HVAC technician should take over.

Frozen evaporator coils are common in humid New Jersey weather. A dirty filter, weak blower, blocked return, or refrigerant problem can cause ice to form inside the system. The AC may run but stop cooling the house. We thaw the system safely, find the cause, and verify airflow before restarting.

An outdoor unit that is not running can point to a bad contactor, failed capacitor, damaged wiring, fan motor failure, or control issue. Air2Cool trucks carry many common repair parts, which helps us complete many emergency AC repairs the same day.
